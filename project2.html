Role

Scope

Fleet Management Platform digitizing vehicle allocation, servicing, tracking, and operational workflows for Ola-owned vehicles.

Users

Fleet Operations Teams · Workshop Staff · Garage Managers · Leadership Teams · Drivers

Challenge

Critical fleet operations relied on paper registers, spreadsheets, emails, and phone calls, resulting in poor visibility, delayed vehicle allocation, and inefficient workflows.

Understanding the Problem

As the lead designer for Ola's Fleet Management platform, I was tasked with improving fleet allocation operations for Ola-owned vehicles.

Through field visits and stakeholder interviews, I discovered that vehicle readiness, servicing, and allocation were managed through disconnected spreadsheets, paper records, emails, and calls. Without a shared source of truth, teams struggled to coordinate efficiently, leading to delays, manual work, and poor operational visibility.

🚗

📊

Daily updates required manually creating spreadsheets and emailing stakeholders — error-prone and time-consuming.

Discovery: Understanding Operations on the Ground

Rather than relying on assumptions, I spent four weeks observing how fleet operations worked across workshops and garages.

Through site visits, shadowing sessions, and interviews with workshop staff, garage managers, fleet operators, and drivers, I mapped the end-to-end vehicle allocation journey and identified where delays and inefficiencies were occurring.

What I Learned

Insight 1: Paper Wasn't the Problem

Teams needed physical handover records for accountability. Removing paper entirely would fail — the goal was digital augmentation, not replacement.

Insight 2: Data Was Entered Multiple Times

Register → Excel → Email. The same information was repeated repeatedly across different formats, creating redundant effort and opportunities for error.

Insight 3: Nobody Had a Complete Vehicle View

Vehicle history — service records, ownership changes, allocation status — lived across people, paper, and systems. No single person had the full picture.

Insight 4: Drivers Had Zero Visibility

Drivers arrived at garages expecting vehicles that weren't ready. No notifications, no updates, no transparency.

Reframing the Challenge

The Problem Wasn't Fleet Allocation.

The real challenge was creating a single source of truth for the entire vehicle lifecycle — one that connected operations, workshops, garages, and drivers.

Before

Improve Fleet Management Tool

Focus on incremental UI improvements to an existing tool.

After

Create an Operational System Connecting People, Vehicles, and Processes

A complete ecosystem transformation — not a feature enhancement.

The reframe: this wasn't a tool improvement project. It was an operational transformation that needed to connect people, vehicles, and processes through a unified platform.

Building for Scale Without Disrupting Operations

Stakeholders agreed with the vision but couldn't stop operations while a large transformation was underway. I proposed a phased roadmap that delivered value incrementally — reducing risk and building momentum with each release.

Phase 1: Digitize Workshop Reporting

Replaced paper registers with mobile-friendly digital reporting — the first step toward real-time visibility.

Phase 2: Fleet Visibility

Gave operations teams real-time visibility into vehicle readiness and allocation status across all garages.

Phase 3: Vehicle History

Built a complete vehicle timeline — service records, ownership changes, allocation history — in a single view.

Phase 4: Driver Tracking

Gave drivers visibility into vehicle readiness and allocation status through notifications and a simple interface.

Phase 5: Unified Fleet Dashboard

A single source of truth for leadership — real-time fleet performance, utilization, and operational metrics.

Elevating the Experience Within Operational Constraints

Users had varying levels of digital literacy. Many workshop operators were transitioning from paper-based workflows for the first time. The design needed to be accessible, not sophisticated.

🧩

The Constraint

Users were comfortable with paper-based workflows but had limited experience with digital tools. Introducing complex enterprise software behaviors would create adoption barriers.

🎯

Design Decision

Prioritized familiar patterns and progressive digitization — starting with digital equivalents of paper registers and gradually introducing more sophisticated capabilities.

🤝

Collaboration

Worked closely with operations teams and engineers to ensure every workflow could be adopted with minimal training — often designing alongside the teams who would use it daily.

🚀

Outcome

High adoption with limited operational disruption. Users transitioned from paper to digital without friction — because the design met them where they were.

Key takeaway: Good enterprise design isn't about adding sophistication. It's about reducing complexity for people doing real work. The most elegant design is the one that disappears into the workflow.

Designing for Operational Reality — Edge Cases That Drove the Design

The platform needed to handle the messy reality of fleet operations — not just the ideal path. These edge cases shaped every design decision.

🔧

Vehicle Assigned but Under Service

A vehicle could be allocated to a driver but still undergoing maintenance — the system needed to flag this conflict.

👥

Multiple Drivers Waiting for Same Vehicle

When demand exceeded supply, the system needed to manage prioritization and communicate wait times transparently.

⏳

Workshop Updates Delayed

Workshop staff working offline needed to sync updates when connectivity returned — without data loss.

📍

Vehicle Transfer Between Locations

Vehicles moved between garages and cities — the system needed to track location and ownership changes seamlessly.

📄

Driver Missing Verification Documents

Incomplete documentation stalled onboarding. The system needed to flag missing documents and guide drivers through completion.

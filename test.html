AI-Powered Dynamic Travel Itinerary Generator

Using AI to transform scattered travel information into personalized, comprehensive itineraries, reducing planning time from hours to minutes.

Strategic Context

Role

Lead Designer & AI Strategist

Industry

Travel Technology

Timeline

48-hour Hackathon

Team

Product Manager, 3 Engineers, Copywriter, Data Analyst

The Core Problem

When travelers search Google for queries like "2 day itinerary for family of 4 in Tuscany Italy", they're overwhelmed with scattered information across dozens of websites. Hotel guides are on one site, restaurant recommendations on another, activity suggestions elsewhere. Each piece of content addresses only one aspect of trip planning, forcing users to manually piece together a complete itinerary across multiple sources.

Key Pain Points Identified

  • • Information fragmentation: Users must visit 10+ sites to plan one trip
  • • Generic recommendations: Content isn't tailored to specific traveler profiles (e.g., "family of 4")
  • • Cognitive overload: Too many choices without clear prioritization
  • • Time-consuming manual synthesis: Users spend hours compiling and organizing information

AI-Augmented Design Process

1

AI-Powered Research & Pattern Discovery

Tools: Google Gemini, NotebookLM

I used Google Gemini to analyze common travel search patterns and user intent. I fed it dozens of real user queries to identify key patterns:

  • Traveler profile: Family size, age groups, travel style (luxury/budget/adventure)
  • Trip parameters: Duration, season, budget range
  • Core needs: Accommodations, dining, activities, transportation, logistics
  • Context-specific requirements: Accessibility needs, dietary restrictions, kid-friendly options

Next, I used Google NotebookLM to synthesize research from top travel sites (TripAdvisor, Lonely Planet, travel blogs) and competitive itinerary builders. NotebookLM helped me extract common content structures, identify information hierarchy patterns, map out content types needed, and analyze gaps in existing solutions.

💡 Key Insight from AI Analysis:

Users need a single, scannable page with clear day-by-day structure, personalized recommendations, and actionable next steps. Generic listicles don't solve the planning problem – context-aware, synthesized itineraries do.

2

Rapid Wireframing with Figma AI

Tools: Figma AI, FigJam

Armed with research insights, I used Figma AI to generate initial wireframe concepts rapidly. Instead of spending hours sketching layouts manually, I prompted Figma AI with structure requirements from NotebookLM.

Figma AI produced 5-6 layout variations in minutes. I refined the most promising concepts, focusing on:

  • Scannable hierarchy: Clear day headers, collapsible sections, visual timeline
  • Personalization indicators: Tags like "Perfect for families" or "Budget-friendly"
  • Actionable design: Prominent booking CTAs, add-to-calendar buttons, share functionality
  • Mobile-first approach: Thumb-friendly navigation, progressive disclosure, fast load times

⚡ Impact:

FigJam brainstorming session aligned the entire team in under 2 hours. Copywriters knew what tone was needed, engineers understood data requirements, and PMs could prioritize MVP features.

3

High-Fidelity Mockups & AI Content

Tools: Figma AI, Google Gemini

With the wireframe validated, I used Figma AI to generate high-fidelity mockups. I iterated on visual design, component consistency (reusable cards for hotels, restaurants, activities), and responsive layouts for mobile and desktop.

I used Google Gemini to generate realistic sample content (hotel descriptions, restaurant reviews, activity details) that matched the tone and length requirements. This made the prototype feel real and helped stakeholders visualize the final experience.

4

Functional Prototype with AI-Generated Code

Tools: Google AI Studio, HTML/CSS/JS

To bring the design to life within the 48-hour deadline, we used Google AI to generate a working HTML prototype. I provided the Figma mockups and design specifications, and AI generated:

  • Semantic HTML structure with accessibility best practices (ARIA labels, keyboard navigation)
  • Responsive CSS matching the design system (flexbox layouts, mobile breakpoints)
  • Interactive JavaScript for collapsible sections, map integration, and booking CTAs

I reviewed and refined the code to ensure WCAG 2.1 AA compliance, performance optimization (lazy loading images, efficient CSS), and design fidelity (pixel-perfect match to mockups).

🎯 Reflection

This project reinforced my belief that AI is a powerful accelerator for designers – not a replacement. By using AI strategically for research synthesis, rapid concept generation, and code prototyping, I freed up time for the work that truly requires human judgment: understanding user needs, making strategic design decisions, and ensuring accessibility and quality.

The key is knowing when to use AI (pattern discovery, generating options, automating repetitive tasks) and when to rely on human expertise (synthesizing insights, making trade-offs, ensuring inclusive design). This balanced approach allowed us to move fast without compromising on craft or user experience.

Case Study: B.xom

Scaling a Moving Train: Building Adoption for 200+ Teams

Joined mid-project to turn a library of parts into a core company asset, reducing development time by 65% while bridging the gap between design standards and product needs.

The Human Challenge

B.xom had the foundations, but we had a "last mile" problem. 200+ product teams were working in silos because the existing system felt too rigid. This wasn't just a design problem; it was an adoption crisis.

The Cost of Fragmentation

  • Engineering Waste: Developers spent 30% of their time rebuilding basic components.
  • Inconsistency: Over 37 button variations across 40+ localized experiences.

The Strategic Playbook

1. From "Police" to "Partners"

I focused on building trust through real-time support. I launched a dedicated help desk and office hours to resolve implementation queries immediately, shifting the team's perception from "gatekeepers" to "enablers."

2. Building for the Extremes

To prove the system's value, I designed the Adaptive Date Picker—our most complex component. It needed to handle ranges and pricing overlays across 40+ locales. By solving this "beast," we reduced implementation time from 3 weeks to 2 hours.

3. Creating a "Watering Hole"

I established a collaborative platform where teams could brainstorm new patterns before they became official. This sense of collective ownership led to an 89% component reuse rate.

"Adoption is the only metric that matters."

A technically perfect library is useless if your support system is a bottleneck. I learned that a design system is 10% pixels and 90% people.

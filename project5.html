The Messy Reality

When I joined Booking.com, the design system was a "moving train." We had components, but teams were working in silos because the system felt like a bottleneck.

The Real Costs

  • • Technical Debt: 37+ variations of a single button across the site.
  • • Wasted Effort: Developers spending 30% of their time rebuilding basic UI.
  • • Low Trust: Teams felt the system was too rigid for their local market needs.

Token Architecture

Semantic Naming Strategy

Instead of:
color-blue-500
We used:
color-background-primary

This abstraction enabled theme switching, accessibility compliance at the token level, and global updates by changing single values.

Component Ecosystem

Foundation Layer

Tokens for colors, typography, spacing, and elevation

Atoms (30+)

Core components maintained by central team

Molecules (50+)

Co-created with product teams: booking cards, flight flows

Example: The "Beast" (Adaptive Date Picker)

The date picker needed to handle ranges and pricing overlays in 40+ locales. I designed it as a modular system with pluggable logic:

  • Headless Logic to handle complex booking rules
  • Token-based Theming for accessibility
  • Slot-based UI for product-specific pricing layers

Result: Implementation time dropped from 3 weeks to 2 hours for product teams.

Cross-Team Brainstorming

I established a rotating Design System Council. We met bi-weekly to brainstorm new product patterns and resolve conflicts. This shifted the culture from "using Jadvinder's library" to "using OUR system."

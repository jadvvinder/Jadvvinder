Driving UX Strategy to Build a Trusted Learning Ecosystem for 1M+ Users

Orchestrating a trust‑first, safety‑driven UX vision that united product, engineering, and content teams, evolving a simple video platform into a holistic learning ecosystem that parents trust and kids love.

Users

Primary: Children aged 1 - 9 years
Secondary: Parents and guardians

Business Challenge

A saturated market of free content (YouTube Kids, learning apps), how to create a paid platform parents would choose?

The Problem Wasn't a Lack of Content

Before building anything, we needed to validate our core assumption. Children already had access to YouTube Kids, educational channels, learning apps, and streaming platforms. The challenge wasn't creating more content, it was understanding why parents still felt dissatisfied with existing solutions.

Critical Questions We Started With

  • • Would parents pay for educational content already available online for free?
  • • What concerns existed around children's screen usage?
  • • What prevented parents from fully trusting existing platforms?
  • • How could we create differentiated value in a highly competitive market?

Rather than jumping into solutions, we started with understanding the problem, deeply, from multiple angles.

Discovery & Research

Designing for children meant designing for two very different user groups. Children and parents often had different goals, motivations, and expectations. The success of the product depended on understanding both.

👨‍👩‍👦

Parent Research

Interviews & moderated sessions exploring screen-time concerns, safety expectations, trust factors, and willingness to pay.

🧒

Child Observation

Observed navigation behavior, attention spans, interaction patterns, engagement triggers, and age-specific needs.

👩‍🏫

Educator Collaboration

Worked with freelance teachers and content partners to define learning objectives, curriculum structure, and age-based progression.

Early interface exploration, testing navigation patterns for young children

Key Insights

Insight 1: Parents Were Not Buying Content

Parents were looking for trust, safety, educational value, age appropriateness, control, and visibility into learning progress. Not another video platform.

Insight 2: Children Needed Simplicity

Traditional navigation patterns created friction for younger users. The experience needed to feel intuitive, visual, rewarding, and age-appropriate.

Insight 3: Learning Outcomes Matter More Than Viewing Time

Parents cared less about how much content children consumed and more about whether meaningful learning was happening. This became the most important strategic driver.

Defining the Product Strategy

Based on research findings, we established three strategic pillars that shaped everything that followed.

🛡️

Safe Learning

Children should only access age-appropriate educational content in a secure environment.

📈

Guided Growth

Content should support developmental learning objectives rather than endless browsing.

💪

Parent Confidence

Parents should feel informed, empowered, and in control of their child's learning journey.

Designing the Learning Ecosystem

The product was designed around two connected experiences, one for children, one for parents.

Child Experience

  • • Educational videos, alphabet, numbers, poems
  • • Engaging interactions & motion
  • • Visual-first, low cognitive load
  • • Large touch targets for younger children

Parent Experience

  • • Track learning progress
  • • Monitor viewing history & engagement
  • • Manage subscriptions
  • • Set usage limits & screen-time controls
  • • Clear, actionable dashboards

Transforming large amounts of behavioral and learning data into dashboards that were easy to understand and act upon, for parents who weren't data analysts.

Launching the MVP

To validate the solution before scaling, we launched an MVP with a focused group of 50 families. The goal was to understand engagement, learning outcomes, parent satisfaction, and product-market fit.

📊

What We Measured

  • • Engagement & daily active usage
  • • Learning outcomes & progress
  • • Parent satisfaction & feedback
  • • Subscription interest & willingness to pay
✅

What We Observed

  • • Strong engagement across age groups
  • • Positive parent feedback
  • • High perceived educational value
  • • Clear subscription interest

Outcome: Based on the success of the pilot, the product moved into broader rollout. A subscription model was introduced to support long-term growth.

When Success Revealed a New Problem

Although engagement and adoption were encouraging, retention began declining after subscription. This created an important question:

Why were users leaving despite positive feedback?

Rather than relying on assumptions, I initiated another round of research.

Follow-up Interviews Revealed an Unexpected Insight

Parents consistently told us: "The content is good."

However, many also shared: "Similar videos already exist on YouTube."

The content itself was not enough to create long-term differentiation.

Another Recurring Theme

Parents wanted their children to spend less time on screens. They appreciated educational content, but they also wanted opportunities for physical activity and offline learning.

This became the breakthrough insight.

The Strategic Reframe

The problem was not content quality. The problem was that we were still competing as a video platform.

If we wanted to create unique value, learning needed to continue beyond the screen.

💡 The Worksheet Experiment

I proposed introducing printable worksheets connected to educational videos.

For Children

  • • Watch educational content
  • • Practice concepts offline
  • • Reinforce learning through physical activities

For Parents

  • • See tangible learning outcomes
  • • Reduce screen dependency
  • • Participate more actively in learning

Influencing Leadership

The proposal initially received pushback. Leadership was focused on improving retention while minimizing investment and operational complexity.

Concerns Raised

  • • Production costs
  • • Logistics & distribution
  • • Scalability
  • • Uncertain impact on retention

Rather than requesting a full rollout, I proposed a low-risk experiment. Using research findings, I aligned stakeholders around testing the concept before making larger investments.

Testing the Hypothesis

We launched a small experiment with approximately 20 users.

To Reduce Risk & Cost

  • • Worksheets were produced internally
  • • Distribution was managed manually
  • • Success measured through retention and engagement

The Objective

Would offline learning increase perceived value and improve retention?

📈 Results Exceeded Expectations

70%
Retention among worksheet recipients
⬆
Significantly higher than baseline
📊
Parents reported higher educational value & satisfaction

The experiment validated the hypothesis. Offline learning wasn't just a nice-to-have, it was the differentiator that parents were looking for.

Scaling the Solution

To minimize operational risk, I recommended a phased rollout strategy, expanding city-by-city before committing to a national launch.

Phase 1: City-by-City Rollout

Expanded gradually to test logistics and gather feedback at each stage.

Phase 2: Subscription Model Adjustment

Bundled worksheet experiences alongside digital content.

Phase 3: Resource Allocation

Additional design resources and new content resources approved as confidence grew.

Phase 4: National Launch

The experience was launched across India as a core part of the product roadmap.

Worksheets became a tangible extension of the digital learning experience, and a key differentiator.
